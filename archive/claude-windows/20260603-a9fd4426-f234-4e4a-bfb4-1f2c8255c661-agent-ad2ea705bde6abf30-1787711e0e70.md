1	# muneem — Design Document
2	
3	> Status: **draft, pre-implementation**. This document captures the vision, the pipeline shape, the security posture, and the open decisions to resolve before code lands. It is intentionally opinionated where the author has a strong view, and explicitly undecided where the trade-offs need a human call.
4	
5	---
6	
7	## 1. What muneem is
8	
9	**muneem** is a personal-finance bookkeeper that ingests financial documents from the user's email accounts, unlocks the password-protected ones, parses them into a normalized schema, stores them in a local database, and surfaces analytics across the user's full financial life.
10	
11	The name is the Hindi word for the traditional household/business bookkeeper — a trusted figure who maintained the family ledger by hand. This project is the same role, automated.
12	
13	**Single user. Local-first. Privacy-critical.**
14	
15	## 2. Why
16	
17	The user's financial life today is fragmented across many silos:
18	
19	- 3–5 bank accounts (statements arrive monthly as password-protected PDFs)
20	- Multiple credit cards (monthly bills, also password-protected PDFs)
21	- Brokerage and mutual-fund accounts (quarterly statements, portfolio snapshots)
22	- Grocery and shopping receipts (Swiggy, Zomato, Amazon, Flipkart, BigBasket, Blinkit — mostly plain HTML/PDF in email body)
23	- Utility bills, insurance premiums, EMI schedules
24	- Promotional offers and coupons (often forgotten)
25	
26	Each silo has its own portal, login, and format. There is no consolidated view. **The inbox is the only place where they already converge.**
27	
28	Concrete use-cases the user wants once this exists:
29	
30	1. **Grocery & shopping price intelligence** — track what was bought, where, and at what price, over time. Detect when a merchant is no longer competitive on staples.
31	2. **Investment portfolio timeline** — a single time-series of holdings across brokers, so allocation drift and rebalancing decisions are obvious.
32	3. **Bank/card comparison** — fees paid, interest earned, reward redemption efficiency, surfaced as objective numbers.
33	4. **Item catalog** — every significant purchase linked to its receipt/invoice, so warranty windows, return windows, and replacement cycles are queryable.
34	5. **Forgotten-deals reminder** — surface the unused coupons and offers already sitting in the inbox (this is the original goal of the predecessor project; see § 11).
35	
36	## 3. Origin
37	
38	muneem is the successor to `~/Projects/Email-Mining`, a small prototype the user built earlier that extracted promo codes from manually-saved email PDFs using PyMuPDF + Gemini for image OCR. Same insight ("the inbox contains structured value I'm forgetting"), generalized scope.
39	
40	See [`docs/prior-art-email-mining.md`](./docs/prior-art-email-mining.md) for what it did, what to inherit, and what not to.
41	
42	## 4. The pipeline (five stages)
43	
44	Each stage is independent enough to develop and test in isolation. They communicate through well-defined data boundaries (raw files on disk → unlocked files on disk → structured records in DB → derived views).
45	
46	### Stage 1 — Email ingestion
47	
48	**Goal:** pull messages and attachments from Gmail and Outlook into a local staging area.
49	
50	- **Gmail:** [Gmail API](https://developers.google.com/gmail/api) over OAuth2. Read-only scope (`gmail.readonly`). Supports server-side search queries (`has:attachment filename:pdf from:hdfc newer_than:90d`) which is far cheaper than pulling everything and filtering locally. Has good rate limits for personal use.
51	- **Outlook:** [Microsoft Graph API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/mail-api-overview) over OAuth2. Read-only scope (`Mail.Read`). Similar search capability.
52	- **Why not IMAP:** worse search, no labels, harder OAuth, and we lose Gmail's powerful query syntax. Use the native APIs.
53	
54	Mechanically straightforward. The main design questions are: **how is "financial document" detected** (sender allowlist? subject patterns? attachment heuristics? hybrid?), and **how is incremental sync state tracked** (Gmail `historyId` / Graph `deltaLink`).
55	
56	### Stage 2 — Attachment unlocking
57	
58	**Goal:** decrypt password-protected PDFs.
59	
60	The decryption itself is trivial — every mainstream PDF library handles the standard security handler. In Go, `github.com/pdfcpu/pdfcpu`; in Python, `pikepdf` or `pypdf`.
61	
62	The real design question is **where the passwords come from.** Indian bank and CC statement passwords are almost always derived from user attributes:
63	
64	- "First 4 letters of name (uppercase) + DDMM of DOB" — common at HDFC, Axis
65	- "PAN number" — common at some brokers
66	- "Last 4 digits of card number" — common for CC statements
67	- "Customer ID" — some banks
68	- "Mobile number" — utilities
69	
70	Three plausible approaches, each with trade-offs:
71	
72	| Approach | Pro | Con |
73	|---|---|---|
74	| **Per-sender rule template** the user configures once (e.g., `from:alerts@hdfcbank.net → first4(name)+ddmm(dob)`) | Deterministic, no guessing, auditable | Requires upfront setup; rules must be maintained as banks change formats |
75	| **Candidate dictionary** (try a small list of derived strings per PDF) | Zero config; "just works" | Brute-forceable feel; fails noisily on unknown senders |
76	| **Interactive prompt** (ask the user the first time, cache for that sender) | Safe, simple | Friction during initial backfill of years of statements |
77	
78	A hybrid is likely best: candidate dictionary first, fall back to rule template, fall back to interactive prompt. **Decision parked.** See § 8.
79	
80	### Stage 3 — Document parsing
81	
82	**This is the hard 80% of the project.** Every issuer's PDF layout is different and changes over time.
83	
84	The space of approaches, in increasing robustness and cost:
85	
86	1. **Per-issuer regex/template parsers.** Precise; brittle. One parser per format, breaks when the issuer redesigns. Realistic for the top ~5 senders that dominate volume.
87	2. **Text-layer extraction + structural heuristics.** Pull text with `pdfminer.six` / `pdfplumber` (both MIT); rely on coordinates and font runs to recover tables. Works well for "born digital" PDFs (most bank statements); fails on scanned/image PDFs. (We avoid PyMuPDF/`fitz` — it is AGPL; see § 5 rule 8.)
88	3. **OCR (Tesseract) for image-based PDFs and embedded raster content.** Slower; need layout reconstruction.
89	4. **LLM-assisted structured extraction.** Hand page text (+ optionally page rasters) to a model with a target JSON schema. Generalizes across layouts; expensive per page; **must be local for sensitive document categories**. Options: Ollama-hosted models (Llama 3, Qwen, Mistral), or a small purpose-built model.
90	
91	In practice this will be **a hybrid**: deterministic template parsers for the top ~5 high-volume senders (where they pay for themselves), LLM-assisted extraction as the long-tail fallback. Promo emails and other non-sensitive categories can use cloud LLMs; bank/card/investment statements **must not** without explicit per-category approval.
92	
93	Embedded image deduplication (Email-Mining used MD5 hash + SSIM) is a real win here: bank statements repeat the same logo/letterhead on every page, and we don't want to OCR that 30 times per document.
94	
95	### Stage 4 — Normalization & storage
96	
97	**Goal:** map heterogeneous parser outputs into one schema, persist locally.
98	
99	Storage: **SQLite**. Single file, no server, easy to back up, easy to encrypt at rest (SQLCipher), good enough performance for years of personal data. The schema needs to span:
100	
101	- `documents` — every ingested file with sender, date, type, hash, path
102	- `accounts` — bank accounts, cards, broker accounts, identified by issuer + last-4 or similar
103	- `transactions` — line items from statements, normalized (date, amount, currency, counterparty, category, source_document)
104	- `holdings` — point-in-time snapshots of investment positions per account
105	- `merchants` — canonicalized merchant identities (so "SWIGGY*BANGALORE", "Swiggy Instamart", and "SWIGGY BNGLR" collapse to one)
106	- `items` — line-items from itemized receipts (grocery, shopping)
107	- `offers` — promotional offers/coupons with expiry, source, redemption status
108	
109	The merchant canonicalization step is non-trivial — it deserves its own thought (fuzzy match + manual override table is the usual answer).
110	
111	### Stage 5 — Analytics & insights
112	
113	**Goal:** turn the normalized DB into the use-cases in § 2.
114	
115	Once the data is in the schema, the analytics are mostly SQL + light application logic. Likely surface: a local CLI initially, possibly a small local web UI later. Notebook-style exploration is fine for prototyping queries before they're promoted to first-class views.
116	
117	---
118	
119	## 5. Security posture (first class)
120	
121	The threat model deserves to be stated plainly: **muneem aggregates the user's entire financial life — every account, every transaction, every holding — into one local store. That store is a honeypot.** A leak would be catastrophic.
122	
123	Standing rules:
124	
125	1. **All raw statement data stays on the local machine.** No cloud sync of the staging dir or the DB.
126	2. **No cloud LLM APIs for sensitive document categories** (bank, CC, broker, MF, insurance) without explicit per-category user approval discussed in the current session. The default extraction path for these is local (Tesseract + local LLM via Ollama, or deterministic parsers).
127	3. **Non-sensitive categories** (promo emails, generic newsletters, public receipts) may use cloud LLMs after a privacy review of the specific content type.
128	4. **DB encrypted at rest** — *implemented* (decision D.2, 2026-05) with **SQLCipher**: transparent AES-256 page encryption via the permissive `sqlcipher3` binding, so SQL queries work unchanged. The 256-bit key is random, lives only in the OS keychain (never in the repo, config, args, or logs), passed in raw-key form. OS full-disk encryption (BitLocker/LUKS) is a recommended complement, not a substitute. See [`docs/encryption-at-rest.md`](./docs/encryption-at-rest.md).
129	5. **OAuth tokens** stored in the OS keychain (Windows Credential Manager / macOS Keychain), not on disk.
130	6. **Logs must not contain extracted statement text.** Sanitize aggressively; redact account numbers, balances, line-items.
131	7. **Nothing sensitive ever in git.** `.gitignore` blocks real PDFs, the DB file, `.env`, and password files. Do not weaken it.
132	8. **Commercial-license safety.** Only dependencies under permissive, commercial-safe licenses (MIT, BSD, Apache-2.0, ISC, HPND). No copyleft (GPL/LGPL/**AGPL**) or source-available licenses. **PyMuPDF/`fitz` is AGPL-3.0 and is banned** — use pdfminer.six (MIT) / pypdfium2 (Apache/BSD) instead. Enforced by `tests/test_dependency_licenses.py`; tracked in [`docs/DEPENDENCIES.md`](./docs/DEPENDENCIES.md).
133	9. **Minimize paid dependencies.** Prefer local / self-hosted / open-source (and, where needed, own-trained) models and tools. Paid APIs — cloud LLMs, paid OCR/vision — are a **fallback only**, gated behind explicit per-use approval. This extends rule 2 from privacy to cost.
134	
135	## 6. Non-goals (for v1)
136	
137	- **Multi-user / SaaS / hosted product.** Single user, single machine.
138	- **Cloud sync of raw data.** Period.
139	- **Cracking unknown passwords.** muneem decrypts PDFs the user is authorized to read, using passwords the user knows or can derive.
140	- **Browser scraping bank portals.** Email APIs only. Portal scraping is a fragile, ToS-fraught rabbit hole; we deliberately avoid it.
141	- **Real-time alerting / push.** Batch-pull on a schedule is sufficient.
142	- **Tax preparation, investment advice, or anything regulated.** muneem surfaces data; the user decides.
143	
144	## 7. Recommended first vertical slice
145	
146	Before generalizing, prove the end-to-end pipeline on the smallest meaningful scope:
147	
148	> **Gmail → one specific bank's monthly statements → unlocked → parsed into `transactions` → stored in SQLite → a CLI command that lists last month's transactions.**
149	
150	This exercises every stage exactly once, on a real input the user cares about, with no detours. It will surface the actual hard parts (which we can only guess at now) and force the open decisions in § 8 to get made on real evidence.
151	
152	Concretely: pick **one** Indian bank statement format the user has many examples of (HDFC, given the user's geography and statement history), build the parser for that one format only, and put the rest of the pipeline around it. Then evaluate before generalizing.
153

Approval creates a consent artefact, a digitally signed record that binds the FIU to the terms it requested. Each artefact is a digitally signed JSON-based object. The FIP validates the artefact, fetches the requested data, encrypts it end to end using the FIU's public key, and passes the encrypted payload back through the AA. ## AA Ecosystem Implementation

The AA ecosystem implements ECC Curve25519 with Diffie Hellman Key Exchange for exchanging information between 2 parties. The process involves FIU (Financial Information User) and FIP (Financial Information Provider) parties:

FIU selects Diffie Hellman parameters and generates a Diffie Hellman key pair (pub_key1, pvt_key1) and a 32 byte nonce (n1), then sends the public component of the key pair, DHParameter and nonce to AA which passes this information to FIP in an encoded format via digitally signed api call.

FIP generates its own key pair (pub_key2, pvt_key2) in the same group specified by FIU and a 32 byte nonce (n2), then uses pub_key1, pvt_key2, n1 and n2 to generate an encryption key (e) and encrypts the data (d).

## General ECDH Overview

ECDH is an anonymous key agreement scheme, which allows two parties, each having an elliptic-curve public–private key pair, to establish a shared secret over an insecure channel. ECDH is a variant of the Diffie-Hellman key exchange that utilizes elliptic curve cryptography, offering strong security with shorter key lengths compared to other asymmetric encryption algorithms.

### PART 1: ACCOUNT AGGREGATOR TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

#### 1.1 Framework Participants & Data Flow

**Three core roles** (RBI Master Directions; Sahamati):

1. **FIP (Financial Information Provider)**: Banks, brokerages, insurance companies — the institutions that *hold* customer data.
2. **FIU (Financial Information User)**: Applications requesting customer data for a specific purpose (e.g., loan underwriting, portfolio aggregation).
3. **AA (Account Aggregator)**: Licensed NBFC-AA acting as a regulated *data-blind intermediary* — it moves encrypted data packets without reading them.

**Data path (high level):**
```
Customer (consent) → AA → FIP (encrypts request) → FIP (encrypts data) → AA → FIU (decrypts with private key)
```

The AA **cannot read, store, or decrypt** customer data. It is a pass-through only.

#### 1.2 Consent Artefact Model (FIU-to-AA-to-FIP)

**Consent is the control gate.** The user explicitly approves a **consent artefact** — a digitally signed JSON object specifying:

- **Purpose**: "Personal portfolio consolidation" vs. "Loan underwriting"
- **Data types**: Account statements, holdings, transaction history, etc.
- **Frequency**: One-time or recurring (e.g., weekly)
- **Validity**: Start/end dates (e.g., 6 months)
- **Expiry post-fetch**: "Retain 30 days, then purge"

**Revocation:** The user can revoke consent in their AA app at any time; the FIU cannot request further data after revocation (RBI Master Directions; Sahamati FAQ).

#### 1.3 End-to-End Encryption: ECDH Key Exchange

**The encryption mechanism is the security spine:**

**Stage 1: FIU generates ephemeral Curve25519 keypair**
- FIU generates a one-time `(public_key_FIU, private_key_FIU)` pair on Curve25519 elliptic curve.
- FIU sends `public_key_FIU` to AA in the consent request.
- Private key **never leaves the FIU**.

**Stage 2: ECDH at FIP**
- FIP receives the consent (with `public_key_FIU`) from AA.
- FIP generates its own ephemeral `(public_key_FIP, private_key_FIP)` pair.
- FIP performs ECDH with `public_key_FIU` and `private_key_FIP` to derive a **shared secret session key**.
- FIP encrypts the customer's financial data using AES-256-GCM with the session key.
- FIP sends encrypted payload + `public_key_FIP` back to AA.

**Stage 3: Decryption at FIU**
- AA passes encrypted payload and `public_key_FIP` to the FIU (AA cannot read it).
- FIU performs ECDH using `private_key_FIU` and `public_key_FIP` to derive the **same session key**.
- FIU decrypts the AES-256-GCM ciphertext locally.

**Critical property:** The AA never sees the session key. Neither FIP nor FIU credentials/passwords are transmitted. The ECDH ensures perfect forward secrecy — each session uses ephemeral keys, and the AA is cryptographically excluded.

- Sahamati technical spec (GitHub: `Sahamati/documentation`)
- Perfios AA encryption guide (ECDH Curve25519 + AES-256-GCM)
- ReBIT technical specifications: [https://specifications.rebit.org.in](https://specifications.rebit.org.in)

**Definitive answer (RBI Master Directions; Sahamati):**

| Actor | Can Access Plaintext? | Why? |
|-------|----------------------|------|
| **AA** | ❌ **NO** | Data-blind intermediary; only transports encrypted packets |
| **FIP** | ✅ **YES (briefly)** | Generates ciphertext from plaintext; but does not retain |
| **FIU** | ✅ **YES** | Only authorized recipient; decrypts locally |

**AA supports this:**

- FIU retention period is explicit ("delete after 30 days").
- RBI requires AAs to support revocation.

**Practical risk:** If muneem is a cloud FIU (e.g., hosted on AWS), the local decryption advantage is lost. **muneem must remain self-hosted or local-only** to preserve privacy benefits of AA.

#### 8.1 AA Adoption Rate

(Source: CGAP; Precisa; CASParser)

(Source: CGAP; Precisa)

- **Multiple AAs exist** (not one central AA).
- A customer's bank account might be on AA #1, insurance on AA #2.
- **No universal interconnection** between AAs.

**Implication:** muneem would need to integrate with *multiple* AA operators (or use a TSP that abstracts multiple AAs). This adds integration burden.

#### 8.3 Common Implementation Pitfalls

(Source: Sahamati; ReBIT specs)

- **Consent lifecycle management:** FIUs often struggle with artefact expiry, auto-renewal, and revocation handling.
- **ECDH key generation:** Improper key handling (reuse, storage in plaintext) has been observed.
- **Audit trails:** Many FIUs maintain insufficient logging.
- **Data retention:** FIUs storing data beyond the consent lifetime is common (though the RBI is tightening this).

**Implication for muneem:** If adopting AA, strict adherence to ReBIT specs and RBI auditing requirements is non-negotiable.

### PART 10: EXPLICIT FINDINGS & RECOMMENDATIONS

#### 10.1 Direct Answers to the User's Questions

**Q: Is AA a viable higher-fidelity alternative to PDF parsing?**

**A: Yes, but not as a replacement — as a complement.**

- **For passive bank/CC statements:** No. PDFs are already working, low-friction, offline-capable. Switching to AA adds operational overhead (consent management, internet dependency) with no benefit.
- **For live portfolio data (holdings, balances):** Yes. AA eliminates parser brittleness and provides structured, auditable data.
- **Conclusion:** PDFs for statements (v1.0); AA for holdings (v2.0+, if live-sync is desired).

---

**Q: Can it preserve local-first / no-mandatory-cloud / no-paid-dependency?**

**A: Partially, with caveats.**

- **Local-first:** ✅ Yes. FIU decryption happens on the user's device (if muneem is self-hosted). AA itself is cloud-based, but the encrypted data is useless to the AA.
- **No-mandatory-cloud:** ⚠️ Conditional. AA requires internet (no offline fallback for live data). PDFs (already in email) are more offline-resilient.
- **No-paid-dependency:** ❌ Uncertain. muneem cannot register as an FIU directly (too small); a **TSP partnership is likely required**, introducing a paid intermediary ($1-5k/month) or revenue-share model. PDFs have no per-transaction cost.
- **Conclusion:** AA is viable *if* a free/cheap TSP is available (e.g., Setu's free tier) and muneem can negotiate exemption from FIU licensing requirements. Otherwise, AA contradicts the no-paid-dependency principle.

---

**Q: Compare attack surface: PDF vs. AA + local decryption.**

**A: Trade-off, not clear win.**

| Threat | PDF | AA + Local Decrypt | Winner |
|--------|-----|-------------------|--------|
| Credential theft | High ❌ (email, PDF password) | Eliminated ✅ | AA |
| In-transit eavesdropping | Medium ⚠️ (email TLS) | Eliminated ✅ (ECDH + AES) | AA |
| Local-machine breach | High ❌ (unencrypted ledger) | High ❌ (same ledger, + private keys) | Tie |
| Issuer data breach | N/A | Same as PDF | Tie |
| Parser brittleness | High ❌ | Eliminated ✅ (structured JSON) | AA |
| Third-party dependency | None ✅ | AA + FIP + TSP ⚠️ | PDF |
| Regulatory oversight | None | RBI audit ✅ | AA |
| Offline capability | Full ✅ | Conditional ⚠️ | PDF |
| **Overall** | 3 wins, 2 losses | 4 wins, 2 losses | **Slight AA edge** |

**Conclusion:** AA is **more secure in aggregate**, but not decisively. The "AA edge" comes from credential elimination + regulatory oversight, not from local decryption (which is equivalent to PDF for at-rest security).

---

**Q: Explicitly flag inferences vs. facts.**

**Facts (from authoritative sources):**
- ✅ RBI Master Directions forbid AA from storing/reading financial data.
- ✅ ReBIT specs mandate ECDH key exchange on Curve25519 + AES-256-GCM.
- ✅ FIU decrypts locally using its private key (ReBIT spec; Sahamati).
- ✅ AA cannot access the shared ECDH session key (cryptographic proof).
- ✅ 2.88B accounts enabled, 38% adoption rate (sources: CGAP, Precisa).
- ✅ RBI requires CISA-certified audits every 2 years (Master Directions).

**Inferences (informed guesses):**
- 🔸 **Inference:** muneem likely cannot register as an FIU without a TSP intermediary (based on FIU licensing requirements for NBFCs/regulated entities; muneem is neither). *Not explicitly verified; assume true unless local fintech regulations clarify.*
- 🔸 **Inference:** TSP integration cost would be ~$1-5k/month for a small app (based on industry pricing for Setu, Finvu, etc.). *Estimated from public docs; exact pricing negotiable.*
- 🔸 **Inference:** A breach of muneem's OS keyring (where ECDH private keys are stored) would expose investment holdings in plaintext (same risk as PDF ledger breach). *Follows from key management best practices; not AA-specific vulnerability.*

#### 10.2 Recommendation for muneem

**Phase 1 (v1.0 — current):** Stick with PDF parsing.
- Lowest friction for passive email-based ingestion.
- Offline-capable.
- No third-party dependencies.
- Reconciliation + local AI/ML is a defensible long-term strategy for format drift.

**Phase 2 (v1.5 — if investment accounts become priority):** Evaluate AA for specific FIP integrations (Groww, Kuvera holdings).
- Use a TSP (e.g., Setu) if cost is acceptable.
- Implement ECDH decryption locally (ReBIT spec).
- Hybrid approach: PDFs for banks, AA for live holdings.
- Maintain local-only storage (no cloud FIU).

**Phase 3 (v2.0+ — if user base grows):** Reassess full AA integration.
- If 10k+ active users, FIU licensing becomes cost-justified.
- RBI compliance becomes a feature, not a burden (privacy assurance for users).
- Live-sync (weekly rebalance suggestions) becomes viable.

**Non-recommendation:** Do not use AA as a **replacement** for PDF parsing of bank/CC statements. The operational friction (consent, revocation, TSP latency) outweighs the technical benefits for passive, monthly-cadence data.

### CONCLUSION

India's Account Aggregator framework is a **structurally sound, RBI-backed alternative to screen-scraping and credential-sharing**. The ECDH encryption, data-blind AA architecture, and consent revocation model are cryptographically and operationally superior to the methods they replaced.

However, for **muneem's specific use-case** (single-user, local-first, passive email ingestion), AA introduces **new operational friction and third-party dependencies** that are not justified for v1.0. **The right move is a hybrid approach:** PDFs for banks/CC (where passive ingestion already works), AA for investment holdings (where live sync is desirable and format diversity is painful).

The privacy posture is **not worse than PDFs** (local decryption preserves the at-rest security model); it is **different** (adds regulatory visibility, eliminates credential risk, but increases third-party trust requirements). For a privacy-first project, this is a **defensible trade-off, not a violation of principles**.

The long-term recommendation: **Build the PDF foundation first; integrate AA as a complement when the user base and use-case justify the operational cost.**
