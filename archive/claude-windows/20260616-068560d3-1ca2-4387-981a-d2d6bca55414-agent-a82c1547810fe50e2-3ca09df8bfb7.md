# C-762/19 CV-Online Latvia v Melons: Holding Summary

## Two-Part Test for Sui Generis Database Right Infringement

The CJEU established a two-part framework for search engine/aggregator liability:

### (a) Extraction and Re-utilisation

The Court found that copying and indexing a database's contents onto one's own servers, then making those contents searchable through a separate interface, constitutes "extraction" and "re-utilisation" within Directive 96/9/EC Article 7. The hyperlinks and metadata reproduction are merely secondary manifestations of this underlying infringement.

### (b) Adverse Effect on Investment + Balancing Test

Critically, extraction/re-utilisation is prohibited only "where those acts adversely affect its investment in the obtaining, verification or presentation of that content"—specifically, where they "constitute a risk to the possibility of redeeming that investment through the normal operation of the database."

The Court balanced competing interests: database makers' need to recoup substantial investment versus users' and competitors' interests in accessing information and innovating. The Court acknowledged that content aggregators benefit the information market through improved searchability and market transparency.

## Operative Ruling

*"Article 7(1) and (2) of Directive 96/9/EC...must be interpreted as meaning that an Internet search engine specialising in searching the contents of databases, which copies and indexes the whole or a substantial part of a database freely accessible on the Internet and then allows its users to search that database on its own website according to criteria relevant to its content, is 'extracting' and 're-utilising' that content within the meaning of that provision, which may be prohibited by the maker of such a database where those acts adversely affect its investment in the obtaining, verification or presentation of that content."*
