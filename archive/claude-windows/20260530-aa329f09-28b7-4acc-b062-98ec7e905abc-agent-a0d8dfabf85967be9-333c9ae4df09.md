# HEVC/H.265 Patent Licensing Summary

## Patent Pools and Licensors

Three main patent licensing bodies operate HEVC licenses:

1. **MPEG LA** - Covers essential patents from 23 companies
2. **HEVC Advance** (also referenced as "Access Advance") - Formally announced March 26, 2015
3. **Velos Media** - Announced March 31, 2017, covers patents from Ericsson, Panasonic, Qualcomm, Sharp, and Sony

Additionally, individual patent holders license directly outside pools.

## Royalty Rates

**MPEG LA (as of September 2014):**
- First 100,000 devices: royalty-free
- Beyond 100,000: "$0.20 per device up to an annual cap of $25 million"
- No content royalties charged

**HEVC Advance (Initial terms, July 2015):**
- Region 1 (US, Canada, EU, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, etc.): "$2.60 per device maximum"
- Region 2 (other countries): "$1.30 per device maximum"
- Content royalty: "0.5% of the revenue generated from HEVC video services"
- No annual caps initially

**HEVC Advance (Revised, December 2015):**
- Region 1: "$2.03 per device maximum"
- Annual caps: "$40 million for devices, $5 million for content, and $2 million for optional features"
- Royalties waived "on content that is free to end users"

**Velos Media (March 2017):**
- Specific rates not detailed in the provided text

## Licensing Complexity vs. AVC

The combined HEVC licensing fees are substantially higher than AVC: "The licensing fees are one of the main reasons HEVC adoption has been low on the web." The article notes that MPEG LA's HEVC fees are approximately double their AVC rates ("$0.10 per device" for AVC vs. "$0.20" for HEVC).

HEVC Advance's initial rates triggered significant industry backlash, described as "unreasonable and greedy" with devices requiring "$2.80" in combined licenses—"twenty-eight times as expensive as AVC."

## Content vs. Decoding/Encoding

## Software Implementations
