**Decoupled Classification Refinement: Hard False Positive Suppression for Object Detection** is a paper by Bowen Cheng, Yunchao Wei, Rogerio Feris, Jinjun Xiong, Wen-mei Hwu, Thomas Huang, and Humphrey Shi.

**Main Contribution:**
The paper analyzes failure cases of state-of-the-art detectors and observes that most hard false positives result from classification instead of localization and they have a large negative impact on the performance of object detectors.

**Key Findings:**
The authors conjecture three factors: (1) Shared feature representation is not optimal due to the mismatched goals of feature learning for classification and localization; (2) multi-task learning helps, yet optimization of the multi-task loss may result in sub-optimal for individual tasks; (3) large receptive field for different scales leads to redundant context information for small objects.

**Proposed Solution:**
The paper demonstrates the potential of detector classification power by a simple, effective, and widely-applicable Decoupled Classification Refinement (DCR) network, which places a separate classification network in parallel with the localization network (base detector). DCR samples hard false positives from the base classifier in Faster RCNN and trains a RCNN-styled strong classifier.

The paper was published as an arXiv preprint (arXiv:1810.04002) in 2018.
