## Overview

SF-Net studies an intermediate form of supervision called single-frame supervision for temporal action localization (TAL), where annotators identify only a single frame within the temporal window of an action. This significantly reduces the labor cost compared to full supervision that requires annotating action boundaries, while introducing extra temporal action signals compared to weak supervision that only annotates video-level labels.

## Key Technical Contributions

The approach proposes to predict an actionness score for each video frame, which along with a typical category score provides information about potential action occurrence and aids temporal boundary refinement during inference. 

SF-Net mines pseudo action and background frames by identifying pseudo action frames through adaptively expanding each annotated single frame to nearby contextual frames and mining pseudo background frames from unannotated frames across multiple videos.

## Results

In experiments on THUMOS14, GTEA, and BEOID datasets, SF-Net significantly improves upon state-of-the-art weakly-supervised methods and achieves comparable results to its fully-supervised counterpart which requires more resource intensive annotations.

The paper explores the proposal-based prediction paradigm for point-level annotations, which has the advantage of more constrained solution space and consistent predictions among neighboring frames. ## Distributional Bias in Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation

Standard implementations of leave-P-out cross-validation (LPOCV) introduce distributional bias—a negative correlation, across folds, between the average label of the corresponding training and test sets. In LOOCV, this approach inadvertently introduces a negative correlation between the average label of the training set and the label of the test instance.

This phenomenon can be demonstrated via a simple predictor that takes advantage of this bias, and in practice, due to the tendency of machine learning models to regress to the mean of the training set labels, this bias tends to decrease common performance evaluation metrics, such as auROC, auPR, or R².

## Rebalanced LOOCV (RLOOCV)

To address this issue, researchers proposed Rebalanced LOOCV (RLOOCV) as a correction. In RLOOCV, for each individual instance that is left out during cross-validation, a sample that has an opposite label to the left-out instance is also omitted from the training set, maintaining a consistent label balance across training instances.

RLOOCV maintains i.i.d. distributions of training and test sets, while LOOCV does not, which ensures that RLOOCV cannot erroneously overinflate performance estimates. This correction consistently increases measured machine learning performances under multiple machine learning benchmarks.
