## The Holding

In June 2021, the Supreme Court ruled in a 6–3 opinion that one "exceeds authorized access" by accessing off-limit files and other information on a computer system they were otherwise authorized to access. More specifically, the Court determined that in order to violate the CFAA, an individual must access an area of a computer or information on a computer that is completely "off limits to him," as opposed to accessing a computer or information that he is entitled to use in at least some circumstances.

## The Case Facts

Nathan Van Buren, a former sergeant with the Cumming, Georgia, police department, used the computer in his patrol car to run a license plate search in exchange for money. Although Van Buren used his own, valid credentials to perform the search, his conduct violated a department policy against obtaining database information for non-law-enforcement purposes.

## The Significance

The CFAA's language had long created a 4–3 circuit split in case law, and this decision narrowed the applicability of CFAA in prosecuting cybersecurity and computer crime. The Court ruled that the CFAA does not prohibit improper use of computer information to which an individual has authorized access; rather, the law prohibits obtaining information from areas of a computer that are outside the limits of the individual's authorized access.

## Key Legal Standards

Courts apply traditional principles of contract law and focus on whether the plaintiffs had reasonable notice of and manifested assent to the clickwrap agreement. Parties must manifest their mutual assent to the terms of the agreement before it can be enforceable.

## Manifestation of Assent

Parties traditionally manifest assent by written or spoken word, but they can also do so through conduct, and if a website offers contractual terms to those who use the site and a user engages in conduct that manifests her acceptance of those terms, an enforceable agreement can be formed.

## Clickwrap Enforceability

Clickwrap agreements involve presenting users with specified contractual terms on a pop-up screen and requiring the user to click or check a box stating "I agree" in order to proceed, and because they involve affirmative conduct manifesting agreement, courts have routinely found clickwrap agreements enforceable.

## Browsewrap Enforceability

Courts are reluctant to enforce browsewrap agreements because users are frequently left unaware that contractual terms were even offered by the company, much less that the user's continued use of a website or app will be deemed to constitute acceptance of those terms.

## Notice Requirements

For browsewrap agreements to be enforceable, a browsewrap agreement must be displayed in a font size and format that a reasonably prudent Internet user would have seen it. Additionally, the consumer has 'inquiry notice,' meaning they could have been informed if they had inquired.
