## Does India Have a Sui Generis Database Right?

No. India does not currently have a sui generis database right comparable to the EU Database Directive. The article explicitly states: "The current copyright regime requires no changes but broadening the scope for interpretation and inclusion of databases in a _sui generis_ manner."

## What Is Protected Under Indian Copyright?

Under Indian law, **only original selection and arrangement are protected—not raw facts or data themselves**.

The Copyright Amendment Act of 1999 included "database" within the definition of "literary work," but Indian courts apply the **"modicum of creativity" test**. This means:

- **Protected**: Creative selection, sequencing, and systematic arrangement of facts
- **Not protected**: Mere facts, raw data, or unoriginal compilations

## Key Case Law

**EBC v. D.B. Modak (2008)** established that "mere arrangement, selection and co-ordination of facts in databases cannot be copyrighted" without demonstrating creativity beyond industrious collection.

## Practical Consequence

Extracting raw facts or data from a database work remains unprotected. Only the creative presentation method itself enjoys copyright protection. The article notes this creates a "free-rider problem" and explains why some advocate for expanded database protection—though the author argues the existing framework is sufficient with broader judicial interpretation.

# DPDP Act 2023: Scope and Key Definitions

## Scope of the Act

The DPDP Act applies specifically to **digital personal data**. According to the legislation, it provides for "the processing of digital personal data in a manner that recognises both the right of individuals to protect their personal data and the need to process such personal data for lawful purposes."

Notably, the Act has a significant limitation: it does not apply to offline personal data. This restriction has drawn criticism, as there is currently no framework governing how offline data is handled when it remains undigitized.

## Definition of Personal Data

Personal data refers to information "by which a person may be identified." The Act distinguishes between data principals (individuals whose data is processed) and data fiduciaries (entities processing that data).

## Anonymized Data

The Act does not cover purely anonymized or aggregated data that cannot be linked to an identifiable individual. Once data is genuinely anonymized, it falls outside the Act's regulatory scope.

## Children's Protections

The legislation includes specific safeguards for minors (under 18 years old):

- Parental consent is required before processing children's data
- The Act "does not permit processing which is detrimental to well-being of children or involves their tracking, behavioral monitoring or targeted advertising"

These provisions represent a comprehensive approach to protecting vulnerable populations in the digital ecosystem.
