### MITIGATION TECHNIQUES (Machine-Verification Aligned)

**Decoding-Time Interventions (no retraining):**

1. **Language-Contrastive Decoding (LCD)**: Suppresses linguistic priors during generation; uses contrastive probabilities between vision and language paths. Reduces hallucinations without model updates. [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04664](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04664)

2. **Visual Grounding Score (VGS)**: Per-token adaptive weighting; amplifies visually grounded tokens, suppresses hallucinated ones. Compares distributions on original vs. distorted images during decoding. [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20314)

3. **Probabilistic Hallucination Detection**: Log-probability and classifier-score fusion for output confidence thresholding. [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.12137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.12137)

**Post-Inference Verification (Your Balance-Check Fallback):**

- **Self-Consistency with Confidence**: Confidence-based filtering improves self-consistency; **P-True method achieves computational cost reduction up to 53%** vs. naive multiple-pass voting. [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06233](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06233)

- **LLM-Based Semantic Evaluation**: Use a second LLM to assess extraction semantics (not just formatting); more reliable than strict BLEU/edit-distance metrics. [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.18652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.18652)

- **Weaknesses:** Heaviest of the top three; base-model table accuracy ~65–75% before FT (Brief 4) — fine, because the reconciler gates output.

### #3 — PaddleOCR-VL (~0.9–1.0B) — **best 8 GB / lightweight pick**

- **Strengths:** Tiny, fast, fully permissive, explicitly handles tables/formulas; ideal if your GPU is 8 GB or you want the cheapest re-sampling loop.
- **Weaknesses:** Smallest capacity → most exposed to the digit/row-alignment failure modes Brief 4 documents; lean public benchmarking on Indian statements **[unverified]**. **Note:** keep the *classic* PaddleOCR (detector+recognizer) Apache too — usable as a plain-OCR feeder.

### #4 — Phi-3.5-Vision (4.2B) — **safe, efficient backup**
- **License:** CODE **MIT** · WEIGHTS **MIT** **[verified]**. https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-3.5-vision-instruct

## 4. CONCRETE RECOMMENDATION

**Prototype two local models as the L3 vision fallback:**

1. **DeepSeek-OCR (3B), MIT — lead candidate.**
   - Cleanest license of any option (MIT code **and** weights, no thresholds), purpose-built for layout/table OCR with grounding output, and small (~7 GB BF16 / ~4 GB Q4).
   - **Realistic setup:** `transformers` + `flash-attn`, BF16 on a **12 GB** card with headroom, or Q4 on **8 GB**. vLLM if you want throughput. Prompt it with the doc-to-markdown + grounding template; post-process markdown rows into `(txn_date, narration, debit, credit, balance)`.

2. **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, Apache-2.0 — robust fallback / fine-tune base.**
   - Most mature, best-documented, and uniquely **fine-tunes to ~92% table accuracy** if you later decide to specialize on HDFC/Axis/ICICI/AU layouts.
   - **Realistic setup:** **16 GB** BF16 native, or **12 GB** at 4-bit (~6 GB). `transformers`/vLLM/Ollama. Pin the **7B** repo — never the 3B (research-license trap).

If the GPU is only **8 GB**, swap the lead pair to **DeepSeek-OCR Q4 + PaddleOCR-VL** (both Apache/MIT, both ~4 GB).

**Suggested architecture:** run DeepSeek-OCR first; if the reconciler fails, re-sample (temperature/self-consistency) a couple of times, then escalate to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, then to human review. The cloud tier stays dark unless explicitly approved.

**Why a probabilistic extractor is safe here — the reconciler is the oracle.** Brief 4 is blunt about the real failure modes: digit transposition, decimal/negative-sign errors, and row/column misalignment are the *most common* VLM errors on financial tables, and multi-turn error cascades can drag accuracy toward ~50%. But muneem's `opening + Σdeposits − Σwithdrawals == closing` check is a **deterministic, non-fuzzy gate**: any digit or alignment error almost always breaks the arithmetic, so a bad extraction is *rejected*, not silently stored. That converts an 80%-ish extractor into a system that is either correct-and-accepted or wrong-and-flagged. Cheap, verifiable mitigations that stack on top:
- **Self-consistency**: sample N times, keep the pass that reconciles (confidence-filtered self-consistency cuts cost ~50% vs naive voting — Brief 4).
- **Per-row vs per-page bookend**: reconcile the running balance line-by-line, not just the page total, to localize the failing row for re-query or human review.
- Optional decoding-time aids (language-contrastive decoding / visual-grounding scoring) exist but are **secondary** — the balance check is your strongest guarantee and should be the gate of record.

**License uncertainties to flag explicitly (do not treat as settled):**
- **DeepSeek-OCR's benchmark numbers** (tables 80.2, etc.) come from a single brief whose *release-date and "v2" claims were wrong*; the **license (MIT) and existence are verified**, but treat its accuracy stats as unconfirmed until you benchmark on your own statements.
- **olmOCR-2** is Apache but carries an AI2 "responsible/research use" *guideline* (not a license clause) — note it, irrelevant for personal use.
- **MinerU** is the most attractive table specialist but its **AGPL dependency (PDF-Extract-Kit)** vs. Apache-wrapper status is **unverified** — needs a real dependency-license audit before adoption; do not assume it's clean.
- **Surya/Marker/Llama-3.2** all carry restricted/custom weights licenses — usable for a personal tool, unsafe as product dependencies.

**Still needs hands-on verification (not resolvable from briefs):**
1. Real accuracy of DeepSeek-OCR vs Qwen2.5-VL-7B on **your actual** HDFC/Axis/ICICI/AU pages (the only benchmark that matters). Use the non-sensitive corpus where possible, real statements locally only.
2. Whether your target GPU is 8/12/16/24 GB — it flips the lead pair (see §1 table).
3. MinerU's commercial-dependency cleanliness, *if* you ever want its best-in-class financial-table accuracy.
