199	    still catches any real trim.
200	    """
201	    errors = []

54	## FAQ
55	
56	### What delivery formats do we accept?
57	Both. A **CVAT export** (image- or track-mode XML — rally fields carried as box
58	attributes) is normalised by `convert-cvat`; an **already-canonical CSV** is taken
59	as-is. Vendors may keep working in CVAT — we do **not** require them to hand-author
60	CSVs.
61	
62	### What is the "canonical" CSV?
63	One row per rally:
64	`rally_number, start_mmss, end_mmss, start_time, end_time, shots_count, ending_reason, last_shot_outcome, score_before, score_after, notes`.
65	`start_time`/`end_time` are **decimal seconds**; `import-local` reads those.
66	
67	### How are timestamps handled? (the fps thing)
68	We recompute each rally's interval from its **frame range × the true fps**
69	(`seconds = frame / fps`). A vendor's CVAT time attributes are **ignored** — a
70	step-sampled job that assumes 30 fps writes systematically wrong times. fps comes
71	from `--fps` or ffprobe of `--video-path`. `mm:ss` is also accepted everywhere.
72	
73	### What does validation check?
74	| code | severity | meaning |
75	|---|---|---|
76	| `overlap` | **blocker** | two rallies' intervals overlap (`end[N] > start[N+1]`) |
77	| `zero_duration` | **blocker** | `end <= start` (single-sample / stray box) |
78	| `frame_out_of_range` | high | a box frame is past the video's last frame |
79	| `shot_density` | medium | long rally with implausibly few shots → likely an **inflated span** (often hides a merged rally) |
80	| `possible_missed_rally` | medium | a long unlabeled gap between two rallies → check for an **omitted rally** |
81	| `ending_reason_vocab` | medium | `ending_reason` outside the agreed set |
82	| `outcome_vocab` | low | `last_shot_outcome` outside `{in,net,out,n_a}` |
83	
84	### Blocker vs. review — what's the gate?
85	`validate-delivery` exits **non-zero if any blocker exists** (so it can gate a bulk
86	loop / CI). Non-blocker issues don't stop the gate but populate the **review sheet**
87	for the human pass. `import-local` independently rejects overlaps/zero-duration, so
88	bad ground truth can't slip into the DB.
89	
90	### How are *missed rallies* caught? (the worst error)
91	Two complementary checks, because a miss shows up two ways:
92	1. **Clean omission** (a real hole in the timeline) → `possible_missed_rally` (gap heuristic).
93	2. **Merged/inflated** (the missed rally got swallowed into a neighbour's box, leaving
94	   no gap) → `shot_density` flags the over-long, under-shot rally, and the human finds
95	   the second rally inside it.
96	Neither is a guarantee — they **surface regions to review**, they don't certify
97	completeness. (A future enhancement: a motion second-opinion over the raw video.)
98	
99	### What's the review sheet?
100	`<id>_review.csv` — one row per rally with `auto_flags` (which checks fired) plus
101	blank `VERIFIED / true_start_mmss / true_end_mmss / true_ending_reason /
102	true_last_shot_outcome / reviewer_comment` columns. Open it next to the video
103	(timestamp overlay on) and fill the flagged rows. This is the human gate that turns
104	a delivery into trusted golden labels.
105	
106	### Why two ending fields (`ending_reason` + `last_shot_outcome`)?
107	They're orthogonal: `ending_reason` is *why* the point ended (winner / forced_error /
108	unforced_error / service_fault / let / other); `last_shot_outcome` is *where* the
109	last shot went (in / net / out / n_a). A shuttle that flew long is
110	`forced_error/unforced_error` + `out` — both kept, no information lost.
111	
112	### How do I onboard a brand-new video?
113	1. Get fps into the intake manifest (we provide it to the vendor).
114	2. `convert-cvat` (if CVAT) → `validate-delivery` → read the report, fill the review
115	   sheet for flagged rallies → correct the CSV → `import-local` → `export`.
116	3. The same suite runs for every video, so quality stays consistent at scale.
117	
118	### How do I pull a single rally out to eyeball it?
119	The slicer (indexer):
120	```bash
121	python -m backend.tools.rally_slicer --video match.mp4 \
122	    --labels golden_labels.csv --rallies 7,16 --out-dir output/rally_clips
123	```
124	Clips are padded to match the highlight compiler (0.5s lead / 1.0s tail).
125	
126	### Does coarse frame sampling hurt boundaries?
127	It depends on the consumer. For **highlight compilation**, ~0.5s sampling slack is
128	absorbed by the compiler's clip padding, so `validate-delivery` doesn't flag sampling
129	density. For **golden training/eval labels** the bar is ±0.3s (Brief §4), and the
130	pickleball/table-tennis pilot showed a 30-frame sampling grid at 59.94 fps puts *every*
131	boundary ≥0.5s off — structurally out of spec. That's why videos are handed off as
132	annotation **proxies** (step 0): light enough to annotate at frame step 1, with the
133	first/last box of each rally on the exact contact/dead frame.
134	
135	### What's an annotation proxy, and why not annotate the original?
136	A 4K/59.94 original is too heavy to frame-step in CVAT — at Phase-B scale (~30-minute
137	videos, >100k frames each) it's not realistic at all, which is what pushed the pilot
138	vendor onto the coarse sampling grid above. `prep-annotation-proxy` re-encodes the
139	source downscaled (≤1080p) with dense keyframes for snappy scrubbing while preserving
140	the **exact frame count, fps, and timeline** (verified after the encode; a failed proxy
141	is deleted). Frame N on the proxy IS frame N on the original, so returned labels apply
142	to the original unchanged. Provenance (source + proxy MD5) is appended to
143	`data/annotation_proxy_manifest.csv` — commit it. The proxy is ONLY for annotation:
144	`import-local`, `export`, and the indexer (which keys videos by file MD5) keep using
145	the original. Full rationale: ADR-002 in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.
146	
147	### Will a non-commercial license block export?
148	The video is **kept** in the DB regardless of license, but the default `export` is
149	**commercial-only** — a non-commercial source (e.g. `CC-BY-NC-SA`) is *excluded*
150	from the eval/training set unless you pass `--include-noncommercial`. So if a video
151	"goes missing" downstream, check its `is_commercial` flag first. (The observability
152	report raises a `license_noncommercial` warning for exactly this case.)
153	
154	### What is the observability report? (`*.report.json` / `*.report.md` / `*.summary.md`)
155	Every `validate-delivery`, `convert-cvat`, and `import-local` run now also writes a
156	per-video **observability report** (next to the video for `import-local`; into the
157	`--out-dir` / next to the canonical CSV otherwise):
158	- **`<id>.report.json`** — machine-readable: every stage, metric, and finding. Diff
159	  two runs to see what changed between deliveries.
160	- **`<id>.report.md`** — the **technical** report: parse / validation / registration
161	  stages plus **findings** (one per delivery issue, each citing this FAQ). Read this
162	  to debug a delivery.
163	- **`<id>.summary.md`** — a **delivery summary** for the vendor / data team: the
164	  rally count and coverage plus the plain-language list of rallies to review — no
165	  internal codes or severities.
166	This is in addition to the existing `<id>_report.md` / `_report.json` / `_review.csv`

## ADR-002 — Annotation proxies are generated in-repo (`curate prep-annotation-proxy`), not by a one-off tool

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-06-12 (owner-ratified)

### Context
The Roora pilot showed the vendor annotates in CVAT directly on our **4K / 59.94 fps
originals** and copes with the decode cost by sampling **every 30th frame**. The result
(pickleball + table-tennis review, 2026-06-10): **every rally boundary off by ≥0.5 s, up
to ~2 s**, against the ±0.3 s bar (Brief §4) — all 24 boundaries sit exactly on the
30-frame grid (~0.5 s at 59.94 fps), so they *structurally cannot* meet spec. The same
grid was flagged `[HIGH]` in the earlier badminton round (`data/roora_badminton_issues.txt`);
it is their standing workflow, not a one-off lapse.

**Phase B makes this untenable, not just suboptimal:** ~12–15 videos **per sport** at
~30 min each (Brief §3). A 30-minute 4K/59.94 file is **>100k frames**; frame-accurate
annotation (frame-step 1) on 4K originals at that scale is not realistic for the vendor.
A lightweight **annotation proxy** (downscaled, scrub-friendly re-encode of the identical
timeline) is therefore close to a **precondition for Phase B**, not an optimization.

The pipeline is proxy-friendly by construction: annotations join by the human-assigned
`video_id` (not file MD5), and `convert-cvat` recomputes all times as `frame / fps`,
ignoring vendor time attributes. The Brief's "do not re-encode" rule binds the *vendor*
(timeline + checksum identity of the file they annotate), not us — provided downstream
processing always runs on the **original** file (see `PIPELINE_ROLE.md`: the indexer keys
by MD5).

### Decision
**We pre-process every video before vendor handoff into an annotation proxy, and the
generator lives in this repo as a `curate prep-annotation-proxy` subcommand** — not a
standalone script, and not in the indexer.

### Proxy contract (invariants the tool must enforce)
- **Same fps as the source — never reduce it.** At a lower fps every CVAT frame index
  changes meaning, and the vendor's 30-frame sampling habit at 30 fps would become a
  **1.0 s** grid — twice as coarse. Same frame count, no trimming, no leading padding →
  proxy seconds map 1:1 onto the original.
- **Downscale 4K → 1080p** (not 720p: table-tennis ball visibility), CRF ~18–20,
  **dense keyframes** (`-g 15`) so frame-stepping/back-scrubbing is fast, **audio kept**
  (contact sound is the annotator's best boundary cue).
- **Verification built in:** assert proxy frame count == original frame count and
  durations match (also surfaces VFR sources, where `frame / fps` is already unsafe).
- **Provenance:** emit/append the intake-manifest row — `video_id`, fps, frame count,
  **original MD5 + proxy MD5** — so the proxy↔original mapping is durable and downstream
  processing provably targets the original.

### Rationale (why the collector, why not a loose script)
- **This repo owns the vendor flow end-to-end** (`docs/annotation/`, intake manifest,
  `convert-cvat` → `validate-delivery` → `import-local`); proxy prep is simply command
  #0 of that existing pipeline, and it already shells out to ffprobe.
- **The ffmpeg invocation is the trivial 20%.** The durable value is the parity
  verification + manifest provenance — exactly what one-off scripts skip. We have
  precedent for the failure mode: unfilled intake-manifest TODOs, eval artifacts lost in
  `%TEMP%`, and indexer predictions once keyed under a *downscaled copy's* MD5.
- **Phase-B frequency:** ~40 runs across sessions/operators → needs tests + CI, i.e.
  repo-resident code.
- **Rejected — standalone script:** acceptable stopgap for an immediate redelivery, but
  no durable verification/provenance. **Rejected — indexer:** it has the re-encode
  primitive (`extract_video_chunk`) but is the product pipeline; the vendor handoff
  workflow, manifest, and ingestion all live here.

### Consequences / follow-ups
- **A proxy alone does not fix boundaries.** It removes the performance excuse; the
  binding fix is the convention from the 2026-06-10 review, to be written into the
  Phase-B brief revision: *annotate at frame step 1; place the first/last box of every
  rally on the exact contact/dead frame; we supply annotation-optimized proxies.*
- **Re-add a grid-snap QA check to `validate-delivery`.** `detect_issues` deliberately
  dropped the `coarse_sampling` flag on the grounds that highlight-compiler padding
  absorbs ~0.5 s slack — valid for highlight *consumption*, invalid for ±0.3 s golden
  *training labels*. A cheap "most boundaries share a common frame stride" flag would
  have auto-caught this delivery.
- Implementation is a separate, owner-gated PR; this ADR records the agreed choice.
