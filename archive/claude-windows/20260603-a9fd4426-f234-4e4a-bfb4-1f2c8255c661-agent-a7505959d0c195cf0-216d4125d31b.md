## Decryption Key Management

The FIP uses a shared secret key to encrypt the financial information it sends, and the FIU uses its private key to decrypt it once received. The key exchange protocol guarantees that no other party, including the AA, can get access to this shared secret key. The FIU receives the encrypted payload and decrypts it locally using its private key.

## Data-Blind Architecture and Self-Custody

The AA never sees the clear-text data at any point. The NBFC-AA never decrypts the data, which is the data-blind principle that makes AAs different from traditional data aggregators. The AA cannot decrypt, read, or store the data.

## Data Privacy Protections

The decrypting of financial data happens on the device of the end customer. Payloads pass through the AA in encrypted form, and only the FIU can decrypt them. This is why the framework is often described as "data-blind." ## RBI Regulation

As per the Reserve Bank of India's Master Directive, Account Aggregators can't be in any other business. They can only be an AA. This prevents an AA from establishing a side business and selling your encrypted data.

The framework prioritizes data privacy by ensuring that AAs remain "data-blind" intermediaries while FIUs maintain exclusive access to decryption keys and unencrypted data.

## Individual Self-Setup and Regulations

Registering with an Account Aggregator (AA) is fully voluntary for consumers. However, it's important to note that the regulations restrict FIU participation to regulated entities.

## FIU Requirements

Currently, only players who are registered and regulated by either of the four Financial Service Regulators (FSR)– RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, are allowed to be FIPs and FIUs. This means individuals cannot self-establish as FIUs on their own.

An FIU must itself be regulated by a financial sector regulator such as RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, or PFRDA, which means the framework never allows raw financial data to flow to an unregulated party.

## How Individuals Can Access AA Services

For individual consumers, the approach is different: Registering for account aggregation with AA is simple - download the AA Android App from Playstore (such as Anumati AA, NADL AA, OneMoney AA, FinVu AA, or CAMSFinservAA), use the mobile number registered with your bank and set a 4-digit PIN, and the bank will verify your mobile number with an OTP.

## Account Aggregator Framework Overview

The Account Aggregator Ecosystem is a consent-based data sharing mechanism that helps an individual securely and digitally access and share information from one financial institution they have an account with to any other regulated financial institution in the AA network.

In summary, while individuals cannot directly set themselves up as FIUs, they can use licensed Account Aggregator apps to share their financial data through the consent-based framework.

### PART I: ACCOUNT AGGREGATOR FRAMEWORK ARCHITECTURE

#### **1.1 Core Technical Model (FACT)**

The AA ecosystem operates on a **"data-blind" architecture**:

- **FIP (Financial Information Provider)** encrypts user data using a shared secret key
- **AA (Account Aggregator, licensed NBFC-AA)** acts as a neutral pipe—cannot decrypt, read, or store financial data at any point
- **FIU (Financial Information User)** receives encrypted payload and decrypts using its private key
- **Key exchange:** Uses Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange protocol; AA never sees the shared secret
- **Data residency:** Entire data path stays in-country (RBI Storage Direction April 2018); combined with India's data localization requirements

*Source: [Sahamati FAQ](https://sahamati.org.in/faq/), [ReBIT specifications](https://api.rebit.org.in/spec/aa)*

**Assessment:** This architecture directly enables muneem's local-first requirement: **encrypted data can be transmitted via AA with decryption happening ONLY on the user's machine.**

#### **1.2 Regulatory & Governance Status (FACT)**

- **Licensed AAs:** Multiple RBI-regulated NBFCs operate as Account Aggregators (Finvu, OneMoney, Anumati, NADL, CAMSFinserv, etc.)
- **FIPs enabled:** 112+ financial institutions live as of Aug 2024; 2.61 billion accounts enabled (Dec 2025)
- **Ecosystem size:** 600+ entities across FIPs, FIUs, AAs, TSPs; 252.9 million linked user accounts (Dec 2025)
- **RBI restrictions:** AA business is SINGLE-PURPOSE—AAs cannot lend, advise, store, or monetize data; leverage ratio capped at 7:1
- **Consent-based:** All data sharing requires explicit user consent via Fair Use Templates (granular, time-limited, purpose-specific)

*Source: [RBI Directions 2025](https://www.fidcindia.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/RBI-PRESS-RELEASE-SRO-AA-12-03-25.pdf), [Sahamati certified entities](https://sahamati.org.in/certified-entities/), [ORF research](https://www.orfonline.org/research/india-s-journey-in-open-banking-consent-control-and-connectivity)*

**The regulatory barrier:** Only regulated entities (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA licensees) can legally be FIUs. Individuals cannot self-establish as FIUs.

- **TSP role:** Technology Service Providers implement ReBIT-mandated APIs with digital signatures + advanced cryptography; cannot bypass encryption

*Source: [DoveRunner RBI compliance guide](https://doverunner.com/blogs/everything-to-know-about-rbi-data-localization-guidelines/), [ReBIT specs](https://specifications.rebit.org.in/artefacts/NBFC-AA_API_Specification_v2.0.0.pdf)*
